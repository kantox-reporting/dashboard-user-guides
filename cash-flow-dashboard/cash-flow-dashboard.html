💸What is the Cash Flow Dashboard?

The Cash Flow Dashboard gives you a clear view of your FX trade cash flows — when amounts are due, how much you are buying and selling per currency, and how your execution rates compare to the weighted average. It helps you plan liquidity, monitor trade activity over time, and review the performance of your hedging programme.

💡 Key Value: See your monthly cash flow picture at a glance — amounts in your preferred reporting currency, with a rate trend line that shows whether your execution rates are improving or deteriorating.

Key Benefits

  • ✓
    Monthly Cash Flow View — visualise settlement amounts by month with execution rate trends overlaid
  • ✓
    Full Trade List — expandable table of all trades with amounts, directions, rates, and liquidity providers
  • ✓
    Dynamic Currency Normalization — view all amounts in your preferred reporting currency (EUR, USD, GBP, CHF, SEK)
  • ✓
    Buy/Sell Split — isolate the buy or sell side of your portfolio

🎛️Filters & Controls

FilterWhat it does
Execution Date (Between)Date range slider and picker — filters all visuals to trades executed within this window
Buy Direction (+/−)Toggle to show positive (buy) or negative (sell) side of your cash flows
Expressed inSelect your normalization currency — all amounts are converted to this currency at current spot rates (EUR, USD, GBP, CHF, SEK)
Entity / AccountFilter to a specific entity or account within your organisation
💡 Tip: Use the "Expressed in" selector to switch to your reporting currency before exporting data for internal reports.

📊Main Cash Flow View

The main page shows your settlement activity with two key elements:

📈 Monthly Bar + Line Chart

A combination chart showing:

  • ■
    Bars (columns): Total normalised amount settled each month — height reflects the volume of FX activity
  • —
    Dashed line (WAHR): Weighted Average Hedge Rate for that month — shows whether your execution rates are trending better or worse over time
Reading the chart: A rising WAHR line on a buy position means you are locking in better rates over time. A falling line may indicate worsening market conditions or a shift in trading mix.
