🔁What is the Standard IHFX Dashboard?

The Standard IHFX (In-House FX) dashboard tracks the complete three-stage flow from K2 subsidiary exposure through to the external bank trade. It shows the subsidiary K2 entries that triggered the flow, the K1 internal hedges created as a result, and the bank trades that ultimately executed the hedging — all linked by a common Internal Hedge Reference.

💡 Key Value: Follow every subsidiary exposure from its submission in the K2 bucket programme, through the internal K1 hedge it triggers, all the way to the external bank trade — with performance metrics and CHF-normalised amounts at each stage.

Key Benefits

  • ✓
    Three-Stage Traceability — K2 subsidiary entry → K1 internal hedge → bank-traded external hedge, all linked via Internal Hedge Reference
  • ✓
    Linked Parent–Child View — each K1 internal hedge (parent) is shown alongside the K2 subsidiary entries (children) that triggered it
  • ✓
    P&L Visibility — Spot P&L and Forward Point Savings shown at the K1 parent entry level, both realised and unrealised
  • ✓
    CHF Normalisation — all amounts are normalised to CHF for cross-currency comparison; an EUR-normalised view is also available
  • ✓
    Status Tracking — status and grouped status shown at every stage so you can see where each hedge stands in its lifecycle

⚙️How It Works

The dashboard reflects a three-stage hedging process spanning the K2 and K1 programmes:

1 — Subsidiary (K2)Submits exposure entries into K2 buckets
→
2 — Bucket ExecutesInternal K1 hedge triggered; HQ receives the exposure
→
3 — HQ (K1)K1 DH programme processes the internal hedge entry
→
4 — External TradeBank trade executed to hedge the K1 exposure in the market

The dashboard captures all three data sets: the Child Entries (stage 1), the Parent Entries (stage 2–3), and the Trade Detail (stage 4). They are linked by the Internal Hedge Reference — a single key that traces the chain from subsidiary entry to external bank trade.

🔍 Internal Hedge Reference: This is the key field that links all three stages. A single Internal Hedge Reference can be associated with one or more child K2 entries (if multiple subsidiary exposures combined into one bucket execution) and one or more bank trades on the K1 side.
⚠️ Status on each stage is independent: A K1 parent entry can be closed while its linked bank trade is still open, or vice versa. Each table shows the status at that specific stage of the flow.

🏢Subsidiary Entries (K2 Child)

The Child Entries table shows the K2 exposure entries submitted by the subsidiary. These are the records that, once the bucket executes, trigger the internal K1 hedge. Rows are grouped by subsidiary entity and entry reference.

FieldDescription
Child EntityThe subsidiary that submitted this K2 exposure entry
Child Entry ReferenceUnique identifier for the subsidiary's K2 exposure entry
Entry DateWhen the subsidiary submitted this entry to the K2 programme
DirectionBuy or sell from the subsidiary's perspective
Amount / CurrencyNotional and base currency of the subsidiary exposure
Counter Amount / Counter CurrencyCounter currency side of the exposure
Value DateSettlement date of the subsidiary exposure
Status / Grouped StatusCurrent lifecycle status of the K2 entry
Total Normalised Amount (CHF)Entry notional converted to CHF for cross-currency comparison
💡 Grouped by parent: Child entries appear grouped under the Internal Hedge Reference of the K1 parent they triggered. Multiple K2 entries can share the same parent if they combined within the same bucket execution.

🏦HQ Internal Hedges (K1 Parent)

The Parent Entries table shows the K1 internal hedge entries created when K2 bucket execution occurred. Each parent entry represents the HQ's exposure to be hedged externally, grouped by Internal Hedge Reference and subsidiary entity.

DirectionBuy or sell from the HQ's perspective

🏛️Trade Detail (Bank Hedge)

The Trade Detail table shows the external bank trades that K1 DH executed to hedge the internal entries. This is the final stage of the flow — the actual market transaction that closes the hedging loop. Rows are grouped by trade reference and linked back to the K1 parent via Internal Hedge Reference.

FieldDescription
Trade ReferenceUnique identifier for the external bank trade
Create DateWhen the bank trade was executed
DirectionBuy or sell from the HQ's perspective
Amount / CurrencyNotional and base currency of the external trade
Counter Amount / Counter CurrencyCounter currency side of the external trade
Value DateSettlement date of the external trade
StatusStatus of the bank trade
Internal Hedge ReferenceThe link back to the K1 parent entry and K2 child entries this trade covers
⚠️ One trade can cover multiple parents: A single bank trade can be linked to more than one Internal Hedge Reference if the K1 DH programme netted or combined multiple internal hedge entries into one external execution.

📈Performance Metrics

Performance metrics are shown at the K1 Parent Entry level. They reflect the programme's results on the HQ side, where the actual hedging against market rates takes place.

MetricMeaning
Spot P&L (Unrealised)Mark-to-market P&L on open K1 entries based on today's spot rate vs. the hedge rate — changes daily until settled
Spot P&L (Realised)Locked-in spot P&L on settled K1 entries
Spot P&L (Total)Sum of realised and unrealised spot P&L
Forward Point Savings (Unrealised)Value of forward points embedded in open K1 positions — the carry benefit or cost of hedging at a term beyond spot
Forward Points Savings (Realised)Locked-in forward point value on settled positions
Forward Points (Total)Sum of realised and unrealised forward point savings
Total (Realised + Unrealised)Overall programme performance: Spot P&L + Forward Points combined
⚠️ Unrealised metrics change daily: Spot P&L (Unrealised) and Forward Point Savings (Unrealised) update with every data refresh as market rates move. They become fixed only once the underlying K1 entry settles.
🔍 Forward Point Savings: Forward points reflect the interest rate differential between the two currencies over the hedge tenor. A positive value means the forward rate is more favourable than spot for the HQ entity; a negative value means the opposite. This is a structural feature of term hedging, not a trading decision.
