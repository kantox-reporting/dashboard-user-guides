🔁What is the Standard IHFX Report?

The Standard IHFX (Internal Hedge FX) report tracks inter-entity FX hedging activity within a multi-entity organisation. When one entity within the group hedges on behalf of another, or when internal hedges are used to transfer FX risk between legal entities, this report provides the audit trail and performance view for those internal transactions.

💡 Key Value: Maintain full visibility over internal FX hedges between entities in your group — amounts, rates, directions, and performance — in one consolidated view.

Key Benefits

  • ✓
    Inter-Entity Transparency — see which entities are the source and destination of internal hedges
  • ✓
    Internal Rate Tracking — compare internal transfer rates against external market rates to ensure fair allocation
  • ✓
    Settlement Visibility — upcoming internal settlements by entity pair and currency
  • ✓
    Full Trade List — every internal hedge transaction with reference, amounts, and counterparty entity

Internal Hedge Transactions Table

The core of the IHFX report is the transaction table, which shows each internal hedge with:

ColumnDescription
Trade ReferenceUnique internal trade identifier
Execution DateDate the internal hedge was booked
Value DateSettlement date between the two entities
Source EntityThe entity whose exposure is being hedged
Counterparty EntityThe treasury or central entity providing the internal hedge
Currency PairThe FX pair for this internal hedge
DirectionBuy or sell from the source entity's perspective
Notional / Normalised AmountOriginal and reporting-currency-converted amounts
Internal RateThe rate agreed between entities for this internal transaction
💡 Internal vs. External rates: The internal rate charged to the source entity typically includes a spread over the external market rate to cover the central treasury's cost of hedging. Review the internal rate vs. the market rate to understand the internal hedging cost allocated to each entity.

🔗K2 to K1 Setup

The IHFX report also covers the K2 to K1 internal hedge setup. In this configuration, K2 programme entries generate offsetting internal hedges that are passed to the K1 programme. This allows the two programmes to share liquidity and hedge positions without creating bilateral external trades.

  • •
    K2 exposure entries create internal hedge requests in the K1 programme
  • •
    K1 executes the external hedge on behalf of both programmes
  • •
    The IHFX report tracks the internal leg of this flow — the K2 → K1 transfer
🔍 For K2 clients: If you are a K2 programme participant, the IHFX report shows the internal hedges your entries generated in K1. The external execution details (bank, rate, settlement) are visible in the K2 DH Report.
